The decision nobody owns.

  • AI is in use across your organization and nobody has been asked to decide what it may do.
  • A transformation has stalled because the leadership team will not commit.
  • A risk reaches the board after it has become a crisis.

In each case the decision was made. It was made by default, and fear made it.

Someone who has sat in those rooms.

Most people hear governance and hear a brake. Jack has spent 25 years in risk and advisory, 15 of them at the Big 4, and what he noticed in the room was the opposite: the leaders who moved fastest were the ones who could name the three controls that let them.

He served as acting enterprise risk and audit leader for a $10B health system, reporting to the C-suite and audit committee.

He led a regulator-driven, board-mandated cybersecurity transformation for a $4B health system that closed its urgent gaps on a $2.5 million budget within one year.

Treat new technology like a new hire.

A job description. A manager. A probation period. A review. You would not let a new hire sign checks on day one, and you do not need to let a new system do it either. Decide, then watch for what moves.

Fewer than one board in five has a plan to educate its directors on AI (NACD, 2025). The gap is not awareness. It is a decision nobody has been asked to own.
