Trusted Advisor: Passing the 2 AM Anxiety Test

If the call comes at 2 AM, you are the advisor. If it comes at 9 AM, you are the vendor.

By Jack P. Flaherty. First published February 2026. Updated October 2026.

It is 2 AM. Your biggest client has just had an operational failure. The supply chain is frozen, the board is on the phone, and the CEO’s own stress is bleeding into his judgement.

Who does he call?

If you run a practice in accounting, advisory or law, the answer to that question sets the trajectory of your firm. If he calls you, not for a deliverable but because he needs someone to think with, you have the rarest standing in professional services. You are a trusted advisor. If he waits until 9 AM and asks for a report, you are a vendor.

What my father knew

My father ran a wholesale printing company. He had no degree and no marketing. He retired comfortably because of two things: his word held, and he met clients where they were. If a client had a crisis, he showed up. I swept his shop floor from the age of ten and watched it work.

Later, on a derivatives trading floor and then across 15 years at the Big Four, I watched firms grow and turn cross-selling into a sales tactic. Pitch the next service to the client you already have. Maximise the account.

That version of cross-selling is broken. Clients can feel it.

The switch

When my first corporate run ended in a layoff while my wife was pregnant, I built a consulting firm from nothing. The market did not care about my resume. It cared whether I could carry its pain. That is when I understood what cross-selling actually is. It is a byproduct of knowing a client’s business so well that you see the next problem before they name it.

Research in the Harvard Business Review found the same thing from the buyer’s side. In B2B relationships, buyers are paying for more than expertise. They are paying for reduced anxiety, trust and a shared vision of where the business is going.1

So stop asking “what else can we sell them?” Ask “where are they hurting, and how do we carry that weight?” When you work that way you stop chasing business. You become the 2 AM call.

Technology is the new 2 AM call

The crisis that tests this now is rarely a supply chain. It is a tool. A client’s team adopted an AI platform last quarter and the audit chair just asked what the firm thinks of it. The partner has an opinion and no position.

That is the moment the vendor and the advisor separate. The vendor sends a brochure. The advisor already knows the client’s business well enough to say: treat it like a new hire. Give it a job description, a manager, a probation period and a review. Decide, then watch for what moves.

Look at your top five clients tonight. If their world cracked open, which of them would call you first? Train your people to be that call. I have always believed in training people to replace me, because a firm of 2 AM advisors is worth more than one.
