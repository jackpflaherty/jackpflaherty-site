PREVIEW

AI and emerging technology, decided like a new hire

Jack Flaherty is the trusted advisor who helps boards and executives make decisions with technology. This year, the technology on the agenda is AI.

The decision nobody owns

AI is already in use across your organization. Some of it was approved. Most of it was not. At some point the board will ask what the controls are, and "we told people to be careful" is not one.

Fear is not a control either. Banning a tool does not stop the work from happening. It moves the work somewhere you cannot see it.

Treat new technology like a new hire

You would never let a new hire start without four things. New technology gets the same four.

A job description

What it is allowed to do, what it is not, and who it serves.

A manager

One named person who answers for it, not a committee.

A probation period

A limited scope, a fixed window and a number you are watching.

A review

Decide, then watch for the ripple, and change the decision when the ripple says to.

That is the whole method. It fits on one page, and a board can ask about it in four questions.

Questions planners ask

Is this an AI keynote or a technology keynote?

Technology, with AI as the example everyone in the room is living with. The method applies to any new system, which is why it still works when the next one arrives.

Does Jack take a position on whether to adopt AI?

Yes. Adopt it the way you would hire someone: on purpose, with a job description, a manager, a probation period and a review. Fear is not a control, and neither is a ban.

How technical does it get?

As technical as the room needs. Directors get the governance questions. Operators get the probation-period mechanics. Nobody gets a tool demo.

Can the board session be combined with the keynote?

Yes. The usual pattern is the keynote for the wider audience and a closed session with the board or executive team the same day.
